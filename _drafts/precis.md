This blog post gives a brief overview of what my undergraduate dissertation *Beauty in Matrixland* for the class Philosophy of Virtual Worlds and Language models is about.

The dissertation is about the interaction between the Simulation Hypothesis, and the Sleeping Beauty Problem. The Simulation Hypothesis is the hypothesis that it is not unlikely that we, the reader and I, are living in a simulation. The Sleeping Beauty Problem is a problem about how we should apportion our credences in the following scenario.

> **(Sleeping Beauty)** Let us be a participant in a scientific experiment. The setup of the experiment is as follows. We will be put to sleep on Sunday. A fair coin is then flipped. If the coin lands Heads, we are woken up on and only on Monday. If the coin lands Tails, we are woken up on both Monday, and Tuesday. When we are woken up, we will be asked what our credence on the proposition that the coin landed Heads is. Once we give our answer, we will be given a pill that both puts us to sleep and erases the memory of us ever been woken up during the experiment. The experiment ends on Wednesday; we will be woken up and notified that the experiment has ended; we will continue with our lives as usual. We know the setup of the experiment. We are woken up and asked what our credence on the proposition that the coin landed Heads is. What should be our answer to the question?

There are two camps when it comes to what our answer to the question should be: Thirders and Halfers. The Thirders believe that we should report that our credence is $\frac{1}{3}$.
